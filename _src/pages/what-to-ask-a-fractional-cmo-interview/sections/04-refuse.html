Ask what they refuse to do

A good fractional CMO can name the work they won't take on, in three kinds. Some work needs execution investment to move a number: media production, PR, press releases. Those channels are not always optimizable on a budget of zero, so a good CMO won't promise results on them without the spend behind them. Some refusals are ethical. I won't misrepresent a product or a brand promise, because that's bad for everyone, the brand included. And some work is simply a misuse of the role, and it should be delegated, freeing the person you're paying to make the decisions.

Then listen for the flip. A candidate who refuses nothing is the red flag here. It usually means one of two things: they don't know the edges of their own capabilities, or they're over-promising to win a job that isn't a good fit. The CMO who names exclusions is showing you self-knowledge. The one who says yes to everything will answer for nothing.
